# NASA HERA Executive Summary

## Moonscape and Robots Planning Phase v1.01

The NASA HERA Moonscape and Robots project is a planning and prototyping effort to enhance the realism of HERA analog missions by adding a lunar exploration environment and robotic systems for mission operations. The project is designed to help HERAnauts experience realistic lunar tasks during their simulated 45-day isolation missions, including terrain navigation, payload handling, and remote operation of robotic assets.

## Purpose

This initiative supports HERA mission fidelity by creating a portable lunar landscape that mimics the challenges of operating in a lunar environment. The project also introduces a robotics component that gives participants realistic mission activities such as pathfinding, transport, manipulation, communication management, and task coordination in a controlled simulation space.

## Core Concept

The system will include:
- An 8 ft × 8 ft portable lunar environment
- Visual barriers and terrain features to maintain immersion
- Four specialized robots with different mission functions
- A Bluetooth-based control and monitoring system
- Mission scenarios for navigation, payload delivery, and remote operations
- A CubeSat or ArduSat transport and handling component

## Key Requirements

### Lunar Landscape
- 8 ft × 8 ft design
- Foldable, portable structure
- Minimum 3 ft wall height
- Crater, rock, and regolith-like terrain features
- Day/night lighting and environmental realism

### Robot System
- Four robots: transport, manipulation, support, and mission operations
- Remote operation over Bluetooth within 100 meters
- Optical and infrared camera capability
- Payload handling for CubeSat-scale objects
- Sensor and data logging support

### Mission Design
- Realistic moon surface navigation and terrain adaptation
- Controlled communication and mission continuity scenarios
- Simulation of obstacles, lighting changes, and emergency procedures
- Safe, realistic, and immersive operations for HERA participants

## Outcome

The final result is intended to provide a realistic and educational space operations environment that supports future prototype development, mission testing, and long-duration analog training. The project will serve as a foundation for the next phase of student prototype development during the 2025-2026 school year.
